# History authoring checklist

Author: Codex, 2026-11-28. Original synthesis of general clinical-education knowledge, not a copied OSCE checklist or a validated clinical protocol. Use for synthetic education cases. Keep uncertain clinical details pending qualified review; these prompts are neither exhaustive nor a rule-out tool.

## Symptom history: SOCRATES / OPQRST

Use either mnemonic to organise the same information; do not count their overlapping domains twice. Author only what serves the learning objectives and a coherent safety assessment.

| Domain | Authorable questions | Disclosure boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Site / region | Where is it now? Where did it begin? | Separate current location from migration. |
| Onset | When did it start? What were you doing? How fast did it reach its worst? | Separate first onset, precipitating activity and onset-to-peak when independently askable. |
| Character / quality | What does it feel like in your own words? | Prefer ordinary language; avoid teaching the patient diagnostic jargon. |
| Radiation | Does it spread elsewhere? | Distinguish current radiation from earlier migration. |
| Associated symptoms | What else occurs with it? Ask targeted safety questions separately. | Split independently askable symptoms and negatives. |
| Timing | Constant or episodes? How long, how often, last episode, getting worse? | Keep duration, frequency and progression distinct. |
| Provocation / palliation | What brings it on, worsens it or helps? | A relieving factor is not proof of a benign cause. |
| Severity | How bad at onset, worst point and now? What can you no longer do? | Record the stated scale and functional impact without inventing precision. |

## Background and patient perspective

- **Past medical/surgical history:** Relevant conditions, prior similar events, operations, admissions and previous investigations. Distinguish a patient's recollection from verified results.
- **Medicines:** Name, dose, route and schedule when authored; indication, recent start/stop/change, adherence, last dose where relevant, nonprescription medicines, supplements and as-needed use. Allow “I cannot recall the dose”; never infer a standard dose from a drug name.
- **Allergies:** Substance and reaction if known, including uncertainty. Do not equate intolerance with allergy or missing history with “no known allergies.”
- **Family:** Relevant illnesses and age at occurrence if authored. Unknown family history remains unknown.
- **Social:** Home support, baseline mobility, occupation, daily activities, smoking, alcohol and other substances, access needs and practical concerns. Avoid demographic stereotypes.
- **Relevant reproductive/sexual history:** Ask sensitively about menstrual timing, bleeding, pregnancy possibility, contraception and sexual history when relevant to anatomy and the complaint. Keep these separately disclosable. A reported history is not a pregnancy-test result.
- **ICE and impact:** Author the patient's ideas about the problem, concerns, expectations and effects on work, sleep, mobility or caring responsibilities. Permit “I don't know what it is”; do not invent a diagnostic belief to fill a template.

## Complaint-specific safety review

Use these as authoring and review prompts. Choose explicit present/absent/unknown states; do not make every feature positive. Keep actual examinations, observations and tests outside patient knowledge unless explicitly supplied to the patient.

### Chest pain

Author current pain status, last episode, episode length and changes in frequency, duration or exertional threshold. Ask about rest episodes, spread to arms/jaw/back, breathlessness, sweating, nausea/vomiting and fainting. Persistent or worsening symptoms, particularly with associated features, warrant safety attention in the learning objectives; short duration or calm delivery alone does not establish safety. Review relevant cardiovascular history and risk factors without making them a diagnosis. These history priorities are supported by [NICE CG95, 1.2.1 and 1.3.1](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg95/chapter/recommendations).

### Headache

Author the onset-to-peak interval, activity at onset, previous comparable headaches and ongoing severity. Review new weakness, altered sensation, speech/vision changes, confusion, reduced consciousness, fever, neck stiffness, vomiting and recent injury. Sudden rapid peak and exercise-triggered onset deserve explicit attention even in a composed patient; reported neurological negatives do not exclude serious pathology or prove a normal examination. See [NICE CG150, 1.1.1–1.1.2](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/cg150/chapter/Recommendations).

### Abdominal / pelvic pain

Review onset, migration, current location, progression, movement-related pain, vomiting, ability to take fluids, bowel and urinary symptoms. Include feverishness and distinguish it from measured temperature. Consider bleeding, fainting/collapse and pregnancy-related history when relevant; severe pain, vaginal bleeding or shoulder-tip pain in a possible pregnancy deserve specific safety attention. Do not infer pregnancy exclusion from demographic labels or call an unperformed test negative. Review [RCOG early-pregnancy pain and bleeding guidance](https://www.rcog.org.uk/for-the-public/browse-our-patient-information/bleeding-andor-pain-in-early-pregnancy/). Other abdominal prompts here are general clinical-education knowledge, not a comprehensive triage algorithm.

### Falls / dizziness in older adults taking anticoagulants

Author posture and activity before the event; dizziness versus spinning versus loss of consciousness; memory of the fall; head strike and other injuries; event timing, time on the floor and ability to get up. Review subsequent headache, vomiting, confusion, drowsiness and new neurological symptoms. Record anticoagulant name, authored dose, schedule and last dose separately from injury; review recent medication changes, adherence, baseline mobility and home support. Do not assume a new tablet proves the cause, or that recalled consciousness excludes intracranial injury. [NICE NG232, 1.5.13](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/NG232/chapter/recommendations) treats anticoagulant use as relevant to imaging consideration after head injury even without other indications; this checklist does not prescribe imaging or treatment. The remaining falls-history prompts are general clinical-education knowledge.

## Review the authored answer

Check chronology, units, plain-language equivalence and disclosure permissions. Mark clinician review as pending when clinical plausibility is uncertain; do not claim guideline compliance from completing this checklist. Review clinical sources again when expanding a case into a new complaint or changing a safety-critical fact.
